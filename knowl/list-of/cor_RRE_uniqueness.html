Corollary 1.12.16. Uniqueness of reduced row echelon form.

Any matrix \(A\) is row equivalent to a unique matrix in reduced row echelon form.
🔗
🔗

Proof.

Let \(A\) be an \(m\times n\) matrix. Using Gauss-Jordan elimination, we can row reduce \(A\) to a matrix \(U\) in reduced row echelon form. Suppose \(A\) is also row equivalent to the matrix \(V\) in reduced row echelon form; then \(U\) and \(V\) are row equivalent, since the row equivalence relation is symmetric and transitive. Thus it suffices to show that if \(U\) and \(V\) are row equivalent matrices in reduced row echelon form, then \(U=V\text{.}\) We do so by induction on \(n\text{.}\) The base step is trivial, since there are only two \(m\times 1\) matrices in reduced row echelon form: namely \(\boldzero_{m\times 1}\) and the column vector with a one in the first row and zeros elsewhere.
🔗
For the induction step we assume that any two row equivalent \(m\times n\) matrices in reduced row echelon form are equal. Let \(U=[u_{ij}]\) and \(V=[v_{ij}]\) be row equivalent \(m\times (n+1)\) matrices in reduced row echelon form, and let \(\boldu_j\) and \(\boldv_j\) denote the columns of \(U\) and \(V\text{,}\) respectively. By Corollary 1.12.14 there is an invertible matrix \(Q\) such that \(QU=U'\text{.}\) The first \(n\) columns of \(U\) and \(V\) form matrices \(U'\) and \(V'\text{,}\) respectively, that are in reduced row echelon form, as one easily checks. Furthermore, \(U'\) and \(V'\) are row equivalent: indeed, since \(Q\boldu_j=\boldv_j\) for all \(1\leq j\leq n+1\text{,}\) we see that \(QU'=V'\text{,}\) and thus \(U'\) and \(V'\) are row equivalent. By the induction hypothesis we must have \(U'=V'\text{,}\) and it remains only to show that \(\boldu_{n+1}=\boldv_{n+1}\text{.}\) We treat two cases: the case where one of \(U\) and \(V\) has a no leading one in its last column, and the case where both matrices have a leading one in their last column.
🔗
In the first case, suppose without loss of generality that the last column of \(U\) does not have a leading one. Consider the linear system with augmented matrix \([U\mid \boldx]\text{.}\) Since the last column of \(U\) is free, it follows from Procedure 1.8.21 that for all \(t\in \R\) there is a solution to this system of the form \(\boldx=(f_1(t),f_2(t),\dots, f_n(t),t)\text{,}\) where \(f_j(t)\) is some function of \(t\text{.}\) Since \(U\) and \(V\) are row equivalent, so are \([U\mid \boldzero]\) and \([V\mid \boldzero]\text{,}\) and thus the two systems corresponding to these augmented matrices have the same solution sets. It follows that for all \(t\in \R\text{,}\) we have \(U\boldx_t=V\boldx_t=\boldzero\text{.}\) Using the column method, we conclude that
\begin{equation*} f_1(t)\boldu_1+\cdots +f_{n}(t)\boldu_n+t\bold_{n+1}=f_1(t)\boldv_1+\cdots +f_{n}(t)\boldv_n+t\bold_{n+1} \end{equation*}
for all \(t\in \R\text{.}\) Since \(U'=V'\text{,}\) we have \(\boldu_j=\boldv_j\) for all \(1\leq j\leq n\text{,}\) and thus conclude that \(t\boldu_{n+1}=t\boldv_{n_1}\) for all \(t\in \R\text{.}\) Taking \(t=1\text{,}\) we conclude that \(\boldu_{n+1}=\boldv_{n+1}\text{,}\) and hence that \(U=V\) in this case.
🔗
Finally, we consider the case where \(U\) and \(V\) both have leading ones in their last columns. Since \(U\) and \(U'\) are in reduced row echelon form, it follows that the leading one in the last column of \(U\) occurs in the row of \(U\) whose corresponding row in \(U'\) is the first zero row of \(U'\text{.}\) Since the same statement mutatis mutandi applies to \(V\) and \(V'\text{,}\) and since \(U'=V'\text{,}\) we see that the leading ones in the last columns of \(U\) and \(V\) occur in the same row. Since all other entries in that last column must be zero, we conclude that \(\boldu_{n+1}=\boldv_{n+1}\text{,}\) and hence that \(U=V\) in this case as well.
