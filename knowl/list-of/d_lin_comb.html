Definition 1.5.5. Linear combination.

Let \(V\) be a vector space, and let \(S\) be a nonempty subset of \(V\text{.}\) A linear combination of \(S\) is a vector of the form
\begin{equation} c_1v_1+c_2v_2+\cdots +c_nv_n\text{,}\tag{1.5.2} \end{equation}
where \(v_i\in S\) and \(c_i\in \R\) for all \(1\leq i\leq n\text{.}\) The scalars \(c_i\) appearing in (1.5.2) are called the coefficients of the linear combination. The linear combination (1.5.2) is trivial if \(c_i=0\) for all \(1\leq i\leq n\text{,}\) and nontrivial if \(c_i\ne 0\) for some \(1\leq i\leq n\text{.}\)
🔗
In the special case where \(S\) is described enumeratively as \(S=\{v_1,v_2,\dots, v_n\}\) or \(S=\{v_1,v_2,\dots\}\text{,}\) we will call a linear combination of \(S\) a linear combination of the \(v_i\) for short.
🔗
We will sometimes use sigma notation for linear combinations, writing
\begin{equation*} \sum_{i=1}^nc_iv_i \end{equation*}
for the linear combination of the vectors \(v_1,v_2,\dots, v_n\) whose \(i\)-th coefficient is \(c_i\text{.}\)
