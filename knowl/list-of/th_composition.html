Theorem 1.21.2 Composition of linear transformations.

Let \(T,T_1,T_2\in \mathcal{L}(U,V)\text{,}\) \(S,S_1,S_2\in \mathcal{L}(V,W)\text{,}\) and \(R\in \mathcal{L}(W,W')\text{.}\)
  1. The composition \(S\circ T\colon U\rightarrow W\) is a linear transformation.
    🔗
    🔗
  2. \(\displaystyle R\circ(S\circ T)=(R\circ S)\circ T\)
    🔗
    🔗
  3. \(S\circ(T_1+T_2)=S\circ T_1+S\circ T_2\) and \((S_1+S_2)\circ T=S_1\circ T+S_2\circ T\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  4. \(T\circ \id_U=T\) and \(\id_V\circ T=T\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
🔗

Proof.
The proofs of (2)-(4) follow pretty much directly from the definitions of the function operations involved and basic set-theoretic properties of functions and their compositions. As such we content ourselves with a proof only of (1). Assume \(T\in\mathcal{L}(U,V)\) and \(S\in \mathcal{L}(V,W)\text{.}\) We use the one-step technique to show the composition \(S\circ T\colon U\rightarrow W\) is linear. Given any \(v,v'\in V\) and scalars \(c,d\in F\text{,}\) we have
\begin{align*} S\circ T(cv+dv') \amp = S(T(cv+dv')) \amp (\text{def. of comp.})\\ \amp =S(cT(v)+dT(v')) \amp (T \text{ linear}) \\ \amp = cS(T(v))+dS(T(v')) \amp (S \text{ linear})\\ \amp =cS\circ T(v)+dS\circ T(v')\text{.} \end{align*}
