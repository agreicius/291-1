Example 1.23.15 Reflection in \(\R^2\).

Let \(T\colon \R^2\rightarrow \R^2\) be reflection through the line \(\ell\) with equation \(y=2x\text{:}\) i.e., \(T\) maps a point \(P=(x,y)\) to its reflection \(P'=(x',y')\) through \(\ell\text{.}\) You may take for granted that \(T\) is linear. (We will have a nice proof of this once we introduce inner product spaces.)
  1. Using the geometric definition of reflection, come up with a basis \(B'=(\boldx_1,\boldx_2)\) such that \(T(\boldx_1)=\boldx_1\) and \(T(\boldx_2)=-\boldx_2\text{:}\) i.e. the reflection \(T\) “fixes” \(\boldx_1\) and “flips” \(\boldx_2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Show that
    \begin{align*} [T]_{B'} \amp = \begin{amatrix}[rr] 1\amp 0 \\ 0\amp -1\end{amatrix}\text{.} \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Use the change of basis formula (1.23.6) to compute \([T]_{B}\text{,}\) the standard matrix of \(T\text{.}\)
