Example 1.4.8. Fields are vector spaces.

Show that a field \(F\) is a vector space, where vector addition is field addition, and scalar multiplication is field multiplication.
🔗
Solution.
The underlying set and operations have already been specified. We add that the field element \(0\in F\) will serve as the zero vector (i.e., we have \(\boldzero=0\)), and for each \(a\in F\text{,}\) its vector inverse will be the field additive inverse \(-a\text{.}\)
🔗
That our proposed vector space operations, zero vector, and vector inverses satisfy the axioms of Definition 1.4.2 now follows essentially immediately from the field axioms satisfied by \(F\text{!}\) For example, the vector space arithmetic axioms (i)-(ii), (v)-(viii) in this context are simply restatements that field addition is commutative and associative, that field multiplication distributes over field addition, that field multiplication is associative, and that \(1\in F\) satisfies the multiplicative identity property. Similarly, the zero vector identity is identical to the additive identity property of fields, giving us axiom (iii), and the vector inverse identity is identical to the additive inverse property of fields, giving us axiom (iv).
