Example 1.7.1. Span of two vectors.

Let \(S=\{v,w\}\subseteq \R^3\text{,}\) where \(v=(1,-2,1)\) and \(w=(3,-2,-1)\text{,}\) and let \(W=\Span S\text{.}\)
  1. Describe visually the set \(W\text{,}\) using our geometric interpretations of vector operations. What sort of geometric is \(W\text{?}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Show that \(W\) can be described as the set of all solutions to the equation \(x+y+z=0\text{:}\) i.e.,
    \begin{equation*} W=\{(a,b,c)\in \R\mid a+b+c=0\}\text{.} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
Solution.
First observe that \(W\) contains \(\Span \{v\}=\ell_1\text{,}\) the line passing through \(\boldzero\) and \((1,-2,1)\text{,}\) and \(\Span \{w\}=\ell_2\text{,}\) the line passing through \(\boldzero\) and \((3,-2,-1)\text{.}\) Next, an arbitrary element \(cv+dw\) of \(W=\Span \{v,w\}\) can be visualized as traveling a along the line through \(v\) a certain distance and direction (determined by the scalar \(c\)), and then traveling in a line parallel to the line through \(w\) a certain distance and direction (determined by the scalar \(d\)). Put another way, starting with the point \(cv\) on \(\ell_1\text{,}\) adding arbitrary scalar multiples \(dw\) of \(w\text{,}\) yields an entire line passing through \(cv\) and parallel to \(\ell_2\text{.}\) This gives rise to a conception of \(W\) as a union of a bunch of lines parallel to \(\ell_2\text{,}\) obtained by translation in the direction \(v=(1,1,1)\text{.}\)
