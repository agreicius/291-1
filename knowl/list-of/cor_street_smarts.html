Corollary 1.18.9. Street smarts.

Let \(V\) be a vector space of dimension \(n\text{,}\) and let \(S\) be a tuple of vectors of \(V\text{.}\)
  1. If \(\len S< n\text{,}\) then \(S\) does not span \(V\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. If \(\len > n\text{,}\) then \(S\) is linearly dependent.
    🔗
    🔗
  3. If \(\len =n\text{,}\) then the following are equivalent:
    1. \(S\) is a basis.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. \(S\) spans \(V\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    3. \(S\) is linearly independent.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
🔗

Proof.

Statements (1) and (2) follow directly from Theorem 1.18.7. Statement (3) is an easy consequence of Theorem 1.18.8. For example, if \(S\) spans \(V\text{,}\) then there is a subset \(S'\) of \(S\) that is a basis of \(V\text{;}\) since all bases of \(V\) have \(n\) elements, and since \(\len =n\text{,}\) we must have \(S'=S\text{;}\) thus \(S\) is a basis. The proof for a linear independent set \(S\) is similar, and is left to the reader.
