Example 1.21.7 Transposition.

Define \(S\colon \ML_{m,n}\rightarrow \ML_{n,m}\) as \(S(A)=A^T\text{.}\) Prove that \(S\) is an isomorphism.
🔗
Solution.
We saw in Example 1.19.15 the the transposition operation is linear. To show it is an isomorphism, we produce an inverse. Define \(S^{-1}\colon \ML_{n,m}\rightarrow \ML_{m,n}\) as \(S^{-1}(B)=A^T\text{.}\) (Note that even though the rule for \(S^{-1}\) is the same as that for \(S\) (i.e., take the transpose), we do not necessarily have \(S=S^{-1}\) as functions since their domains (and codomains) are not necessarily equal.) We have
\begin{align*} S\circ S^{-1}(B) \amp =S(B^T)=(B^T)^T=B \text{ for all } B\in \ML_{n,m}\\ S^{-1}\circ S(A) \amp =S^{-1}(A^T)=(A^T)^T=A \text{ for all } A\in \ML_{m,n}\text{,} \end{align*}
proving that the two maps are indeed inverses of one another. We conclude that \(S\) is invertible, and hence an isomorphism.
