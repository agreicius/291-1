Corollary 1.14.4. Determinant and products of elementary matrices.

Let \(A\in \ML_{n,n}(F)\text{,}\) and suppose we have
\begin{equation} B=E_rE_{r-1}\cdots E_1\, A\tag{1.14.6} \end{equation}
for some collection of elementary matrices \(E_i\text{.}\) Then
\begin{equation} \det B=\det E_r\, \det E_{r-1}\cdots \det E_1\, \det A\text{.}\tag{1.14.7} \end{equation}
🔗
🔗

Proof.

This is an easy proof by induction on the number \(r\) of elementary matrices involved. The base case is actually covered by Theorem 1.14.2 (the properties (1)-(3) and the determinant formulas), since we have
\begin{align*} \det \underset{c\, r_i}{E}A \amp =c\det A=\det \underset{c\, r_i}{E}\det A \\ \det \underset{r_i\leftrightarrow r_j}{E}A \amp =-1\det A=\det \underset{r_i\leftrightarrow r_j}{E}\det A \\ \det \underset{r_i+cr_j}{E}A \amp =\det A=\det \underset{r_i+cr_j}{E}\det A \text{.} \end{align*}
