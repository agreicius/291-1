Example 1.7.8. Visualizing planes in \(\R^3\).

Let \(\mathcal{P}\subseteq \R^3\) be a plane defined by the nontrivial equation
\begin{equation} ax+by+cz=d\text{.}\tag{1.7.3} \end{equation}
We will describe two methods of visualizing \(\mathcal{P}\text{.}\) The first, more elementary approach might be described as the triangle method. This entails first finding by inspection three non-colinear points \(P,Q,R\) satisfying equation (1.7.3). The plane \(\mathcal{P}\) is then visualized by sketching the triangle \(\triangle PQR\) in \(\R^3\) they define, and envisioning \(\mathcal{P}\) as the plane in \(\R^3\) containing this triangle. In practice we can find three such points by picking arbitrary values for two of the three variables, and solving for the third variable using (1.7.3).
🔗
For example, if \(\mathcal{P}\) is the plane with equation \(x+y+z=2\text{,}\) we see easily that \(P=(2,0,0), Q=(0,2,0), R=(0,0,2)\) lie on \(\mathcal{P}\text{,}\) allowing us to produce the graph in Figure 1.7.9.
🔗
Leaning on your multivariable calculus background, we also recall a method of visualization using normal vectors. Since the equation (1.7.3) is nontrivial, the vector \(\boldn=(a,b,c)\) is nonzero and is called a normal vector to the plane \(\mathcal{P}\text{.}\) The normal vector method for visualizing \(\mathcal{P}\) proceeds as follows: pick any point \(P=(x_0,y_0,z_0)\in \mathcal{P}\text{,}\) and visualize \(\mathcal{P}\) as the set of all points \(Q=(x,y,z)\) such that the vector \(\overrightarrow{PQ}=(x-x_0, y-y_0, z-z_0)\) is orthogonal to \(\mathcal{n}\text{.}\) This allows us to identify \(\mathcal{P}\) as the plane passing through \(P\) and orthogonal to \(\boldn\text{.}\)
🔗
Note: by invoking orthogonality, we might be accused of having gotten ahead of ourselves somewhat.
