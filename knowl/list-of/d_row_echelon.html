Definition 1.8.5. Row echelon form.

A zero row of a matrix, is a row whose entries are all equal to zero; a nonzero row is a row that contains at least one nonzero entry.
🔗
A matrix \(A\in \ML_{m,n}(F)\) is in row echelon form if the following conditions hold.
  • Zero rows at bottom.
    If the \(i\)-th row of \(A\) is a zero matrix, then for all \(i\leq j\leq m\text{,}\) the \(j\)-th row of \(A\) is also a zero row: i.e., all zero rows are grouped at the bottom of \(A\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  • Leading ones.
    For all \(1\leq i\leq m\text{,}\) if the \(i\)-th row of \(A\) is nonzero, then the first (i.e., leftmost) nonzero entry of this row is equal to \(1\text{.}\) Such an entry is called a leading one of the row: i.e., it is the first nonzero entry of the row, and is equal to \(1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  • Staircase pattern.
    For all \(1\leq i< i'\leq m\) if the \(i\)-th and \(i'\)-th rows of \(A\) are nonzero, then the leading one of the \(i'\)-th row lies strictly to the right of the leading one in the \(i\)-th row. In more detail: if the leading ones of the two rows are the \(i,j\)-th and \(i',j'\)-th entries of \(A\text{,}\) respectively, then \(j< j'\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
A matrix is in reduced row echelon form if it is in row echelon form and additionally satisfies the following condition.
  • Reduced row echelon form.
    For all \(1\leq j\leq n\text{,}\) if the \(j\)-th column of \(A\) contains a leading one, then all other entries of that column are equal to zero.
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
A linear system \(L\) is in row echelon form (resp. reduced row echelon form) if its augmented matrix is in row echelon form (resp. reduced row echelon form).
