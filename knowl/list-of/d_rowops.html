Definition 1.8.8. Elementary row operations on matrices.

An elementary row operation is one of the three following types of matrix operations. In what follows, we let \(A=[a_{i,j}]_{m\times n}\in \ML_{m,n}(F)\text{.}\)
  • Scalar multiplication.
    Given \(1\leq i\leq m\) and nonzero \(c\ne 0\in F\text{,}\) multiply all entries of the \(i\)-th row by \(c\text{:}\) i.e., replace \(a_{i,j}\) with \(ca_{i,j}\) for all \(1\leq j\leq n\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  • Row swap.
    Given \(1\leq i, j\leq m\text{,}\) swap the \(i\)-th and \(j\)-th rows of \(A\text{:}\) i.e., replace \(a_{i,k}\) with \(a_{j,k}\) and \(a_{j,k}\) with \(a_{i,k}\) for all \(1\leq k\leq n\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  • Row addition.
    Given \(1\leq i,j\leq m\) and \(c\in F\text{,}\) replace the \(i\)-th row of \(A\) with the sum of the \(i\)-th row and \(c\) times the \(j\)-th row: i.e., replace \(a_{i,k}\) with \(a_{i,k}+ca_{j,k}\) for all \(1\leq k\leq n\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
The act of transforming a matrix using elementary row operations is called row reduction.
🔗
Two matrices are row equivalent if the one can be obtained from the other by performing a finite sequence of elementary row operations.
