Corollary 1.21.12 Finite-dimensional spaces.

Let \(V\) and \(W\) be finite dimensional vector spaces. The following statements are equivalent.
  1. \(V\) is isomorphic to \(W\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. \(\dim V=\dim W\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
In particular, if \(V\) is an \(n\)-dimensional vector space, then \(V\) is isomorphic to \(F^n\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Assume \(V\) and \(W\) are finite-dimensional vector spaces.
🔗
If \(V\) and \(W\) are isomorphic, then there is an isomorphism \(T\colon V\rightarrow W\text{.}\) By Theorem 1.21.11, if \(B=(\boldv_i)_{i=1}^n\) is a basis of \(V\text{,}\) then \(B'=T(B)=(T(v_i))_{i=1}^n\) is a basis of \(W\text{.}\) In particular, we have
\begin{equation*} \dim V=\len B=\len B'=\dim W\text{.} \end{equation*}
🔗
Assume \(\dim V=\dim W=n\text{.}\) By definition, this means that there are bases \(B=(v_1,v_2,\dots, v_n)\) and \(B'=(w_1,w_2,\dots, w_n)\) of \(V\) and \(W\) respectively. By Theorem 1.19.21 there is a unique linear transformation \(T\in \mathcal{L}(V,W)\) satisfying \(T(v_i)=w_i\) for all \(1\leq i\leq n\text{.}\) We claim that \(T\) is an isomorphism. Indeed, there is a unique linear transformation \(S\in \mathcal{L}(W,V)\) satisfying \(S(w_i)=v_i\) for all \(1\leq i\leq n\text{,}\) and we claim that \(S=T^{-1}\text{.}\) To see why note that the linear transformations \(S\circ T\) and \(T\circ S\) satisfy
\begin{align*} S\circ T(v_i)\amp= S(w_i)=v_i \text{ for all } 1\leq i\leq n \\ T\circ S(w_i)\amp= T(v_i)=w_i \text{ for all } 1\leq i\leq n \text{.} \end{align*}
But then \(S\circ T(v_i)=\id_V(v_i)\) for all \(v_i\) in the basis \(B\text{,}\) showing that \(S\circ T=\id_V\) by the uniqueness claim in Theorem 1.19.21; similarly, \(T\circ S(w_i)=\id_W(w_i)\) for all \(w_i\) in the basis \(B'\) implies \(T\circ S=\id_W\text{.}\) This proves that \(S=T^{-1}\text{,}\) and hence that \(T\) is an isomorphism.
