Theorem 1.14.1. Determinant is alternating and multilinear.

Let \(F\) be a field, let \(n\) be a positive integer, and let \(V=F^n\text{.}\) We consider \(\det\) as a function on \(V\) by defining \(\det(\boldr_1,\boldr_2,\dots, \boldr_n)\) as \(\det A\text{,}\) where \(A=(\boldr_1,\boldr_2,\dots, \boldr_n)\text{.}\)
  1. Alternating property.
    If \((\boldr_1,\boldr_2,\dots, \boldr_n)\in V\) satisfies \(\boldr_i=\boldr_j\) for some indices \(i\ne j\text{,}\) then \(\det(\boldr_1,\dots, \boldr_n)=0\text{.}\) In terms of matrices, if a matrix \(A\) has two identical rows, then \(\det A=0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Multilinear property.
    Given any \((r_i)_{i=1}^n\in V^n\text{,}\) and index \(1\leq k\leq n\text{,}\) and any vector \(\bolds_k\in F^n\text{,}\) and scalar \(c\in F\text{,}\) we have
    \begin{align} \det(\boldr_1,\dots, \boldr_k+\bolds_k,\dots, \boldr_n)\amp =\det(\boldr_1,\dots, \boldr_k,\dots, \boldr_n)+\det(\boldr_1,\dots, \bolds_k,\dots, \boldr_n)\tag{1.14.1}\\ \det(\boldr_1,\dots, c\boldr_k,\dots, \boldr_n)\amp =c\det(\boldr_1,\dots, \boldr_k,\dots, \boldr_n)\text{.}\tag{1.14.2} \end{align}
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
🔗

Proof.

The alternating property (statement (1)) is true vacuously for \(n=1\text{,}\) and can be easily verified for \(n=2\) via a direct computation, and then can be easily proved by induction for all \(n\geq 2\) by expanding along a row of the matrix \(A\) that is not equal to \(\boldr_i\) or \(\boldr_j\text{.}\) We leave this to the reader.
🔗
We can prove the multilinear properties of (2) directly by expanding along the \(k\)-th row of the corresponding matrix. In more detail, let \(A=(\boldr_1,\boldr_2,\dots, \boldr_n)\text{,}\) where \(\boldr_i=(r_{i,1},\dots, r_{i,n})\) for all \(i\text{,}\) and let \(B=(\boldr_1,\dots, \bolds_k, \dots, \boldr_n)\text{,}\) where \(\bolds_k=(s_{k,1},s_{k,2},\dots, s_{k,n})\text{.}\) Letting \(C=(\boldr_1,\dots, \boldr_k+\bolds_k,\dots, \boldr_n)\text{,}\) we have
\begin{align*} \det(\boldr_1,\dots, \boldr_k+\bolds_k,\dots, \boldr_n) \amp = \det C\\ \amp = \sum_{j=1}^n (-1)^{k+j}(r_{k,j}+s_{k,j})\det C_{k,j}\\ \amp = \sum_{j=1}^n (-1)^{k+j}r_{k,j}\det C_{k,j}+\sum_{j=1}^n (-1)^{k+j}s_{k,j}\det C_{k,j}\\ \amp = \sum_{j=1}^n (-1)^{k+j}r_{k,j}\det A_{k,j}+ \sum_{j=1}^n(-1)^{k+j}s_{k,j}\det B_{k,j} \\ \amp = \det A+\det B\\ \amp =\det(\boldr_1,\dots,\boldr_k,\dots, \boldr_n)+\det(\boldr_1,\dots, \bolds_k,\dots, \boldr_n) \text{,} \end{align*}
where we have used the fact that \(C_{k,j}=A_{k,j}=B_{k,j}\) as these three matrices are identical except for their \(k\)-th rows.
🔗
Similarly, we have
\begin{align*} \det (\boldr_1,\dots, c\boldr_k,\dots, \boldr_n) \amp = \sum_{j=1}^n(-1)^{k+j}cr_{k,j}\det A_{k,j}\\ \amp = c\sum_{j=1}^n(-1)^{k+j}r_{k,j}\det A_{k,j} \\ \amp = c\det(\boldr_1,\boldr_2,\dots, \boldr_n) \text{.} \end{align*}
