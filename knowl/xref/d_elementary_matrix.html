Definition 1.11.16. Elementary matrices.

An elementary matrix \(E\in \ML_{m,m}(F)\) is a matrix of one of the three types below.
  • Given \(1\leq i\leq m\) and nonzero scalar \(c\in F\text{,}\) the scaling elementary matrix\(\underset{c\,r_i}{E}\in \ML_{m,m}(F)\) is the matrix whose \(i,i\)-th entry is \(c\text{,}\) whose \(j,j\)-th entry is \(1\) for all \(j\ne i\text{,}\) and whose other entries are all zero.
    🔗
    🔗
  • Given distinct indices \(i,j\in \{1,2,\dots, m\}\text{,}\) a row swap elementary matrix\(\underset{r_i\leftrightarrow r_j}{E}\) is the matrix obtained from the \(m\times m\) identity matrix by swapping the \(i\)-th and \(j\)-th rows.
    🔗
    🔗
  • Given \(1\leq i\leq m\) and any \(c\in F\text{,}\) the row addition elementary matrix\(\underset{r_i+c\,r_j}{E}\) is defined as
    \begin{equation*} \underset{r_i+c\,r_j}{E}=I_m+cE_{i,j}\text{.} \end{equation*}
    That is, \(\underset{r_i+c\,r_j}{E}\) is the result of taking the \(m\times m\) identity matrix and making the \(i,j\)-th entry equal to \(c\text{.}\)
