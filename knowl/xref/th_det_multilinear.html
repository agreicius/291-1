Theorem 1.14.1. Determinant: alternating,skew-symmetric, multilinear.

Let \(F\) be a field, let \(n\) be a positive integer, and let \(V=F^n\text{.}\) We consider \(\det\) as a function on \(V^n\) by defining \(\det(\boldr_1,\boldr_2,\dots, \boldr_n)\) as \(\det A\text{,}\) where \(A=(\boldr_1,\boldr_2,\dots, \boldr_n)\text{.}\)
  1. Alternating property.
    If \((\boldr_1,\boldr_2,\dots, \boldr_n)\in V\) satisfies \(\boldr_i=\boldr_j\) for some indices \(i\ne j\text{,}\) then \(\det(\boldr_1,\dots, \boldr_n)=0\text{.}\) In terms of matrices, if a matrix \(A\) has two identical rows, then \(\det A=0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Multilinear property.
    Given any \((r_i)_{i=1}^n\in V^n\text{,}\) and index \(1\leq k\leq n\text{,}\) and any vector \(\bolds_i\in F^n\text{,}\) and scalar \(c\in F\text{,}\) we have
    \begin{align} \det(\boldr_1,\dots, \boldr_i+\bolds_i,\dots, \boldr_n)\amp =\det(\boldr_1,\dots, \boldr_i,\dots, \boldr_n)+\det(\boldr_1,\dots, \bolds_i,\dots, \boldr_n)\tag{1.14.1}\\ \det(\boldr_1,\dots, c\boldr_i,\dots, \boldr_n)\amp =c\det(\boldr_1,\dots, \boldr_i,\dots, \boldr_n)\text{.}\tag{1.14.2} \end{align}
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Skew-symmetric.
    Suppose \(1\leq i< j\leq n\text{.}\) We have
    \begin{equation} \det(\boldr_1,\dots, \boldr_j,\dots, \boldr_i,\dots, \boldr_n)=-\det(\boldr_1,\dots, \boldr_i,\dots, \boldr_j,\dots,\boldr_n)\text{.}\tag{1.14.3} \end{equation}
    In terms of matrices, if \(B\) is the result of swapping two rows of a matrix \(A\text{,}\) then \(\det B=-\det A\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
🔗

Proof.

  1. The alternating property is true vacuously for \(n=1\text{,}\) and can be easily verified for \(n=2\) via a direct computation. An induction argument then easily proves the result for all \(n\geq 2\) by expanding along a row of the matrix \(A\) that is not equal to \(\boldr_i\) or \(\boldr_j\text{.}\) We leave this to the reader.
    🔗
    🔗
  2. We can prove the multilinear properties of (2) directly by expanding along the \(k\)-th row of the corresponding matrix. In more detail, let \(A=(\boldr_1,\boldr_2,\dots, \boldr_n)\text{,}\) where \(\boldr_i=(r_{i,1},\dots, r_{i,n})\) for all \(i\text{,}\) and let \(B=(\boldr_1,\dots, \bolds_i, \dots, \boldr_n)\text{,}\) where \(\bolds_i=(s_{k,1},s_{k,2},\dots, s_{k,n})\text{.}\) Letting \(C=(\boldr_1,\dots, \boldr_i+\bolds_i,\dots, \boldr_n)\text{,}\) we have
    \begin{gather*} \det(\boldr_1,\dots, \boldr_i+\bolds_i,\dots, \boldr_n) =\det C=\\ \sum_{j=1}^n (-1)^{k+j}(r_{i,j}+s_{i,j})\det C_{i,j}=\\ \sum_{j=1}^n (-1)^{k+j}r_{i,j}\det C_{i,j}+\sum_{j=1}^n (-1)^{k+j}s_{i,j}\det C_{i,j}=\\ \sum_{j=1}^n (-1)^{k+j}r_{i,j}\det A_{i,j}+ \sum_{j=1}^n(-1)^{k+j}s_{i,j}\det B_{i,j}=\\ \det A+\det B=\\ \det(\boldr_1,\dots,\boldr_i,\dots, \boldr_n)+\det(\boldr_1,\dots, \bolds_i,\dots, \boldr_n) \text{,} \end{gather*}
    where we have used the fact that \(C_{i,j}=A_{i,j}=B_{i,j}\) as these three matrices are identical except for their \(k\)-th rows.
    🔗
    Similarly, we have
    \begin{align*} \det (\boldr_1,\dots, c\boldr_i,\dots, \boldr_n) \amp = \sum_{j=1}^n(-1)^{k+j}cr_{i,j}\det A_{i,j}\\ \amp = c\sum_{j=1}^n(-1)^{k+j}r_{i,j}\det A_{i,j} \\ \amp = c\det(\boldr_1,\boldr_2,\dots, \boldr_n) \text{.} \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
  3. The skew-symmetric property now follows from the alternating and multilinear properties. Given \(1\leq i< j\leq n\text{,}\) we have
    \begin{gather*} 0 =\det(\boldr_1,\dots, \underset{i-\text{th entry}}{\boldr_i+\boldr_j},\dots, \underset{j-\text{th entry}}{\boldr_i+\boldr_j},\dots, \boldr_n) \text{,} \end{gather*}
    by the alternating property. Expanding out the right side of the equation above using the multilinear property in succession yields
    \begin{gather*} \det(\boldr_1,\dots,\boldr_i,\dots, \boldr_i,\dots,\boldr_n)+\det(\boldr_1,\dots, \boldr_i,\dots, \boldr_j,\dots, \boldr_n)\\ +\det(\boldr_1,\dots,\boldr_j,\dots, \boldr_j,\dots,\boldr_n) +\det(\boldr_1,\dots, \boldr_j,\dots, \boldr_i,\dots, \boldr_n) \\ =\det(\boldr_1,\dots, \boldr_i,\dots, \boldr_j,\dots, \boldr_n)+\det(\boldr_1,\dots, \boldr_j,\dots, \boldr_i,\dots, \boldr_n)\text{,} \end{gather*}
    since
    \begin{equation*} \det(\boldr_1,\dots, \boldr_i,\dots,\boldr_i,\dots, \boldr_n)=\det(\boldr_1,\dots, \boldr_j,\dots, \boldr_j,\dots, \boldr_n)=0 \end{equation*}
    by the alternating property again. In all, we have
    \begin{equation*} 0=\det(\boldr_1,\dots, \boldr_i,\dots, \boldr_j,\dots, \boldr_n)+\det(\boldr_1,\dots, \boldr_j,\dots, \boldr_i,\dots, \boldr_n)\text{,} \end{equation*}
    or equivalently,
    \begin{equation*} \det(\boldr_1,\dots, \boldr_j,\dots, \boldr_i,\dots, \boldr_n)=-\det(\boldr_1,\dots, \boldr_i,\dots, \boldr_j,\dots, \boldr_n)\text{.} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
