Example 1.17.3. Some standard bases.

It is easy to show that for each \(V\) below, the given \(B\) is a basis, which we will call the standard basis of \(V\text{.}\)
  1. \(V=\{\boldzero\}\text{,}\) \(B=()\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. \(V=F^n\text{,}\) \(B=(\bolde_1,\bolde_2,\dots, \bolde_n)\)
    🔗
    🔗
  3. \(V=\ML_{m,n}(F)\text{,}\) \(B=(E_{i,j})_{(i,j)\in I\times J}\text{,}\) where \(I=\{1,2,\dots, m\}, J=\{1,2,\dots, n\}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  4. \(V=F[x]_{\deg\leq n}\text{,}\) \(B=(1,x,\dots, ,x^n)\)
    🔗
    🔗
  5. \(V=F[x]\text{,}\) \(B=(1,x,x^2,\dots)=(x^k)_{k=0}^\infty\)
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
