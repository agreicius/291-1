Theorem 1.19.23. Matrix transformations.

Let \(m\) and \(n\) be positive integers.
  1. Matrix transformations are linear.
    Given any \(A\in \ML_{m,n}(F)\text{,}\) the function \(T_A\colon \R^n\rightarrow \R^m\) is a linear transformation.
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Transformations from \(\R^n\) to \(\R^m\).
    If \(T\colon \R^n\rightarrow \R^m\text{,}\) there is a unique matrix \(A\) such that \(T=T_A\text{.}\) In fact, we have
    \begin{equation} A=\begin{bmatrix}\vert\amp \vert\amp \amp \vert \\ T(\bolde_1)\amp T(\bolde_2)\amp \cdots \amp T(\bolde_n)\\ \vert\amp \vert\amp \amp \vert \end{bmatrix}\text{.}\tag{1.19.2} \end{equation}

    🔗

Proof.

  1. This was shown in Example 1.19.14.
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Let \(B=\{\bolde_1,\bolde_2,\dots, \bolde_n\}\) be the [cross-reference to target(s) "rm_standard_bases" missing or not unique] of \(\R^n\text{.}\) Let \(A\in \ML_{m,n}(\R)\) and let \(\bolda_j\) denote the \(j\)-th column of \(A\) for all \(1\leq j\leq n\text{.}\) We have
    \begin{align*} T=T_{A} \amp \iff T(\bolde_j)=T_A(\bolde_j) \text{ for all } 1\leq j\leq n \amp ([cross-reference to target(s) "th_bases_transformations" missing or not unique] , [cross-reference to target(s) "th_transf_unique" missing or not unique] ) \\ \amp \iff T(\bolde_j)=\bolda_j \text{ for all } 1\leq j\leq n \text{,} \end{align*}
    where the last equality follows from the column method of matrix multiplication:
    \begin{equation*} A\bolde_j= \begin{bmatrix}\vert\amp \vert\amp \amp \vert \\ \bolda_1\amp \bolda_2\amp \cdots \amp \bolda_n\\ \vert\amp \vert\amp \amp \vert \end{bmatrix}\colvec{0\\ \vdots \\ 1 \\ \vdots \\0}=1\bolda_j\text{.} \end{equation*}
    It follows that \(T=T_A\) if and only if \(A\) is the matrix whose \(j\)-th column is \(T(\bolde_j)\) for all \(1\leq j\leq n\text{.}\) In other words,
    \begin{equation*} A=\begin{bmatrix}\vert\amp \vert\amp \amp \vert \\ T(\bolde_1)\amp T(\bolde_2)\amp \cdots \amp T(\bolde_n)\\ \vert\amp \vert\amp \amp \vert \end{bmatrix} \end{equation*}
    is the unique \(m\times n\) matrix satisfying \(T=T_A\text{.}\)
